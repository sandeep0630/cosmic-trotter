Who Is Lord Venkateswara of Tirupati?

తిరుమల వేంకటేశ్వర స్వరూపం • The Lord of the Seven Hills

Tirumala Ananda Nilayam — where the supreme Lord stands in eternal Archavatara for humanity.

The Lord Who Dwells Upon the Serpent Hill

High upon the eastern crest of the Deccan plateau, amidst the dense forests of the Eastern Ghats in Andhra Pradesh, rise seven sacred mountain peaks known collectively as Seshachalam or Saptagiri. Nestled within these emerald peaks lies Tirumala, home to the ancient shrine of Lord Venkateswara — known affectionately across India and the globe as Srinivasa, Balaji, Venkataramana, Govinda, and Edukondalavadu (The Lord of the Seven Hills).

In Vedic theology and Vaishnava tradition, Lord Venkateswara is not merely an earthly deity or a localized guardian spirit; He is the supreme preserver Lord Vishnu Himself, manifest on Earth in a self-revealed stone icon (Swayambhu Archavatara). The name Venkateswara carries profound spiritual power: Vem signifies the burning away of all human sins, Kata signifies the bond of earthly karma, and Ishwara denotes the Supreme Lord. Thus, He is "The Supreme Lord Who Dissolves Every Sin of Those Who Seek Refuge in Him."

Traditional layer: Skanda Purana (Venkatachala Mahatmya) & Varaha Purana; verified through TTD Agama tradition.

The Vow of Archavatara in Kali Yuga

Unlike previous avatars such as Rama or Krishna who walked the Earth during Treta Yuga and Dvapara Yuga and then returned to their celestial abode of Vaikuntha, Lord Venkateswara made an eternal cosmic vow: to remain physically present on Earth throughout the entire duration of Kali Yuga (the current cosmic age of strife and spiritual shadow).

In this dark age, when direct meditation (*Dhyana*) or elaborate Vedic sacrifices (*Yajna*) are difficult for ordinary human beings to perform, the Lord takes the form of Archavatara — a physical deity accessible to human eyes, ears, and touch. Here on Venkatadri, He accepts human tears, hair offerings, small coins, and simple prayers, transforming ordinary human effort into transcendent grace.

Discourse insight: Sri Chaganti Koteswara Rao Pravachanam emphasizes the supreme accessibility (*Saulabhya*) of Sri Venkateswara over cosmic transcendence (*Paratva*).

The Synthesis of Majesty and Mercy

Eminent spiritual master Brahmasri Chaganti Koteswara Rao and scholar Sri Nanduri Srinivas highlight that the icon of Lord Venkateswara at Tirumala uniquely integrates the majesty of Lord Vishnu with the supreme compassion of Goddess Sri Lakshmi. Standing four-armed in the sanctum, His upper hands hold the divine Conch (Sankha, representing the cosmic sound Om) and the fiery Discus (Chakra, representing protection and time).

His lower right hand is held in Varada Mudra (pointing downward toward His divine feet), assuring every seeker: "Surrender your burdens at My feet and fear nothing." His lower left hand is resting upon His hip in Kati Mudra, signifying that for those who trust in Him, the ocean of worldly suffering (*Samsara*) will only reach knee-deep!

Core Wisdom
  • • Venkata: The divine fire that burns away human karma and suffering.
  • • Archavatara: God making Himself physically present for Kali Yuga humanity.
  • • Kati Mudra: The assurance that worldliness remains only knee-deep for true devotees.
  • • Saulabhya: Supreme divine accessibility that accepts even simple clay offerings.

The Cosmic Cause of Descent

వైకుంఠ పరిత్యాగం & భృగు మహర్షి పరీక్ష • Leaving Vaikuntha for Earth

Vaikuntha — Bhrigu Maharshi tests the supreme Satva of Lord Vishnu.

The Great Yajna at Naimisharanya

The cosmic narrative behind Lord Venkateswara’s arrival on Earth begins at the dawn of Kali Yuga in the sacred forest of Naimisharanya. The great assembly of sages, led by Kulapati Saunaka Maharshi, initiated a grand millennium Yajna for the spiritual protection and peace of humanity. However, a crucial question arose among the sages: To which of the Trimurtis — Brahma, Shiva, or Vishnu — should the principal offerings (*Yajna Phala*) be dedicated?

To resolve this doubt, the assembly requested Bhrigu Maharshi, one of the mind-born sons of Brahma and a sage of formidable austerity, to visit the celestial realms of the three gods and examine who possessed pure *Satva Guna* (divine serenity and humility) untainted by anger or pride.

Traditional layer: Padma Purana & Skanda Purana; recited extensively in Chaganti Garu's discourses.

Bhrigu’s Test of the Trimurtis

Bhrigu first traveled to Brahmaloka. Lord Brahma, immersed in scholarly pride and discourse with Goddess Saraswati, did not stand up or offer greeting to Bhrigu. Indignant at this neglect, Bhrigu departed and proceeded to Kailasa. There, Lord Shiva was engaged in cosmic dance with Goddess Parvati. When Shiva approached to embrace Bhrigu, the sage turned away, declaring Shiva to be breaking decorum. Enraged, Shiva raised His trident, but Parvati pacified Him, and Bhrigu departed unhurt.

Finally, Bhrigu entered Vaikuntha, the supreme abode of Lord Vishnu. He found Vishnu reclining peacefully upon the coils of the thousand-headed serpent Adisesha, while Goddess Lakshmi gently pressed His feet. Determined to push the test to its extreme limit, Bhrigu stormed into the sanctum and deliberately kicked Lord Vishnu directly on His chest — the very place where Goddess Lakshmi resides as *Srivatsa*!

The Foot Press and Bhrigu's Enlightenment

Instead of flying into a rage, Lord Vishnu immediately rose from Adisesha with profound sweetness and humility. Taking Bhrigu’s feet in His hands, the Lord gently massaged them, asking with tender concern: "O great Sage, is your delicate foot injured by striking my hard chest?"

As Vishnu softly pressed Bhrigu's foot, He subtly pressed and neutralized a third eye of spiritual ego located in the sole of Bhrigu’s foot. In that electric instant, Bhrigu's pride dissolved into tears of ecstasy. Realizing that Vishnu alone possessed absolute Satva Guna, infinite patience, and supreme love, Bhrigu bowed in total devotion and declared Vishnu as the supreme lord worthy of all Yajna offerings.

Goddess Lakshmi’s Departure to Kolhapur

However, this cosmic event unleashed an unexpected consequence. Goddess Lakshmi (*Sri Devi*) was deeply offended that Bhrigu had struck the chest of her Lord — her eternal dwelling place — and that Vishnu had welcomed the sage with affection rather than punishing him. Unable to tolerate the insult to her abode, Lakshmi left Vaikuntha in righteous indignation and descended to Earth, taking up solitary penance at Karavirapura (modern-day Kolhapur in Maharashtra).

Desolate without Goddess Lakshmi, the divine light left Vaikuntha. Lord Vishnu, unable to endure the emptiness of Vaikuntha without His eternal consort, descended from heaven to Earth in mortal guise, wandering as a lonely prince named Srinivasa until He reached the sacred Seshachalam hills.

Fact-check note: Discourse analysis by Nanduri Srinivas clarifies that Lakshmi's departure was not mere human jealousy, but a divine cosmic leela planned to bring Vishnu to Earth to save Kali Yuga humanity.

Core Wisdom
  • • True divine greatness is recognized by humility, patience, and love under provocation.
  • • Spiritual ego (symbolized by Bhrigu's foot eye) must dissolve before true darshan.
  • • Cosmic leela transforms celestial conflict into earthly salvation for humanity.
  • • Vaikuntha descends to Earth when the Lord seeks His beloved Sri.

Seshachalam & The Pact with Sri Varahaswamy

వరాహ క్షేత్ర ఒప్పందం & వకుళమాత • The Seven Hills and the First Offering

Swami Pushkarini — Lord Varahaswamy granting land on Venkatadri to Lord Srinivasa.

Saptagiri — The Seven Sacred Peaks

When Prince Srinivasa arrived on the Seshachalam range, He found a landscape charged with ancient spiritual vibrations. Tradition records that the seven hills are the physical body of the cosmic serpent Adisesha, who descended to Earth to serve as the mountain throne of Vishnu. Each of the seven peaks represents a distinct cosmic power:

  • Seshadri: The peak of Adisesha, representing eternal shelter.
  • Neeladri: Named after Neela Devi, the deity who offered her hair for the Lord (origin of hair offering).
  • Garudadri: The peak of Garuda, the celestial eagle vehicle of Vishnu.
  • Anjanadri: The birthplace of Lord Hanuman, son of Anjana Devi.
  • Vrishabhadri: The peak where Nandi (Vrishabha) performed penance.
  • Narayanadri: The peak where Sage Narayana meditated.
  • Venkatadri: The central peak where Lord Venkateswara stands today.

The Sacred Pact with Lord Adi Varahaswamy

In Satya Yuga, after defeating the demon Hiranyaksha and lifting Goddess Bhudevi from the cosmic ocean, Lord Adi Varahaswamy (Vishnu’s Boar Avatar) chose the Seshachalam range as His earthly abode, resting beside the sacred lake Swami Pushkarini. Thus, the entire mountain range legally and spiritually belonged to Lord Varahaswamy!

When Prince Srinivasa arrived seeking a place for His anthill residence, He approached Lord Varahaswamy. Varahaswamy agreed to grant Srinivasa one hundred paces of land near Swami Pushkarini, but established an immutable cosmic contract:

The Pact of Tirumala: "Every pilgrim who ascends this hill must first seek the darshan of Sri Adi Varahaswamy and offer Him the first Naivedyam (food offering) before approaching Lord Venkateswara. If any pilgrim skips Varahaswamy, their pilgrimage remains incomplete!"

To this day, TTD temple tradition honors this pact without exception: the morning *Naivedyam* is offered first at the Sri Varahaswamy shrine on the banks of Swami Pushkarini before the sanctum doors of Lord Venkateswara receive food offerings.

Mother Vakula Devi — Yashoda’s Rebirth

To care for Srinivasa on the mountain, Lord Varahaswamy sent an elderly saintly woman named Vakula Devi to act as Srinivasa's foster mother. Puranic history reveals a beautiful thread of divine affection: Vakula Devi was none other than Yashoda, the loving mother of Lord Krishna in Dvapara Yuga!

During Krishna Avatar, Yashoda had grieved deeply because she was unable to witness any of Krishna's divine marriages to Rukmini or Satyabhama. Lord Krishna had promised her: "Mother, in my next avatar as Srinivasa in Kali Yuga, you shall be my mother once again, and you alone shall arrange my royal wedding with your own hands!" On Venkatadri, this promise was fulfilled as Vakula Devi built an ashram and showered motherly love upon Srinivasa.

Core Wisdom
  • • Varaha First: Respecting prior sanctity and protocol is essential in spiritual life.
  • • Yashoda's Rebirth: Unfulfilled pure devotion in one lifetime finds glorious fulfillment in another.
  • • Saptagiri Map: The mountain itself is a living scripture of seven divine hoods.
  • • Swami Pushkarini: The sacred lake containing waters brought from all cosmic holy rivers.

The Divine Wedding of Srinivasa & Padmavati

శ్రీనివాస పద్మావతి కల్యాణం & కుబేర అప్పు • Love, Vow, and Celestial Loan

Narayanavanam — the glorious celestial marriage of Srinivasa and Padmavati.

Akasa Raja & The Golden Lotus Birth

In the kingdom of Tondaimandalam (near present-day Narayanavanam), ruled the virtuous King **Akasa Raja** and Queen Dharani Devi. Though blessed with righteousness and prosperity, the royal couple longed for a child. Advised by royal preceptors, Akasa Raja initiated a grand Yajna for progeny. While plowing the sacred sacrificial ground with a golden plowshare, the blade struck a golden vessel buried in the earth.

Inside the vessel lay a breathtaking thousand-petaled golden lotus, upon which rested a radiant infant girl. Akasa Raja named her **Padmavati** (She Who Emerged from the Lotus). Puranas reveal that Princess Padmavati was the incarnation of **Vedavati** — the righteous saintly woman of Treta Yuga who had been promised by Lord Rama that she would marry Him in Kali Yuga when He descended as Srinivasa.

Traditional layer: Bhavishyottara Purana & Srinivasa Kalyanam Mahatmya.

The Forest Encounter & Erukula Sani Disguise

One morning, while hunting a wild elephant in the lush gardens near Narayanavanam, Prince Srinivasa encountered Princess Padmavati playing with her maidens. Their eyes met, and mutual divine love blossomed instantly. Returning to His ashram on Venkatadri, Srinivasa fell ill with divine longing, refusing food and rest.

To speed the alliance, Lord Srinivasa assumed the disguise of an old fortune-teller woman (Erukula Sani or Siddha Swarnamukhi) carrying a wicker basket and a bamboo rod. Visiting the palace of Queen Dharani Devi in Narayanavanam, the fortune-teller read Princess Padmavati's palm and proclaimed: "Your daughter is no mortal girl; she is Sri Lakshmi herself! She is destined to marry Prince Srinivasa of the Venkatadri hills!" Soon after, Mother Vakula Devi arrived at the palace as an official matchmaker, formally sealing the royal alliance.

The Celestial Loan from Kubera

The Celestial Bond — Kubera granting gold to Srinivasa with Lord Brahma and Shiva writing the deed.

As preparations began for a marriage fit for the entire cosmos, a practical challenge arose: Srinivasa, living as an ascetic prince on the mountain, possessed no gold or jewels to pay for the wedding feast, gifts, and bridal ornaments required by royal custom. To resolve this, Lord Vishnu summoned Kubera, the celestial treasurer of the gods.

Lord Srinivasa borrowed fourteen lakh Ramamudra gold coins (vast celestial wealth) from Kubera to conduct the wedding. A formal legal loan document was drafted: **Lord Brahma and Lord Shiva acted as divine witnesses**, signing the bond! The covenant specified that Lord Srinivasa would pay back the annual interest on the loan to Kubera until the end of Kali Yuga, while the principal would be repaid at the dissolution of the age.

Living tradition: This divine bond forms the spiritual background of the Tirumala Hundi. Devotees deposit gold, coins, and currency into the Hundi to assist the Lord in paying the interest on His wedding loan, receiving multiplied divine blessings in return!

The Wedding at Narayanavanam & Agastya Vow

On a radiant auspicious day, the wedding of Lord Srinivasa and Princess Padmavati was celebrated at **Narayanavanam** with incomparable grandeur. Lord Brahma, Lord Shiva, Indra, the Dikpalakas, celestial apsaras, and countless sages attended. Following the wedding, Sage Agastya requested the divine couple to stay at his ashram at **Agastya Ashram (Chandragiri / Swarnamukhi riverbank)** for six months.

Sage Agastya established a sacred injunction: "Newly wedded couples should not immediately climb the sacred Venkatadri hill for six months following their marriage."

Clarification by Sri Nanduri Srinivas: In his discourse on film misconceptions, Nanduri Srinivas explains that Agastya Maharshi placed this rule to prevent worldly honeymooning or householder attachment from muddying the pristine ascetic atmosphere of the holy hill. Tirumala is a place of intense spiritual focus, not worldly pleasure.

Core Wisdom
  • • Divine Promise: Vedavati's patience across ages culminates in Padmavati's wedding.
  • • Kubera Loan: The Lord willingly enters the economy of human debt to invite human participation.
  • • Hundi Devotion: Giving to the Lord's Hundi is an act of joining His cosmic household.
  • • Agastya Rule: Sacred places demand spiritual purity and self-restraint over worldly indulgence.

Manifestation as Archavatara & The Five Idols

పంచబేరాలు & గర్భాలయం • The Sanctum Sanctorum and Five Deities

Dhruva Bera — Lord Venkateswara with Sri Devi on His right chest and Padmavati on His left chest.

Reunion at Tiruchanoor & Transformation into Stone

Following the six-month stay at Agastya Ashram, Goddess Lakshmi arrived from Kolhapur at **Tiruchanoor** (Padmavathi Temple / Swarnamukhi river bank). There, Goddess Lakshmi (*Sri Devi*) met Goddess Padmavati. Realizing that both were manifestations of the one supreme Mother, they embraced with profound joy.

Together with Srinivasa, they ascended the Venkatadri hill. To fulfill His vow to protect humanity until the end of Kali Yuga, Lord Srinivasa entered a divine meditation and transformed Himself into the majestic, black granite self-manifested deity (Dhruva Bera or Moola Virat). Goddess Sri Devi merged into the right side of His chest (**Vaksha Sthalam**), and Goddess Padmavati merged into the left side of His chest. Thus, Lord Venkateswara became the eternal shelter where Lord and Goddess dwell as one undivided light!

The Pancha Beras — Five Deities of the Sanctum

Inside the golden *Ananda Nilayam* sanctum at Tirumala, worship is not conducted for one isolated icon alone. As detailed in Agama manuals and highlighted by Sri Nanduri Srinivas in his lectures on Tirumala secrets, there are **Five Sacred Deities (Pancha Beras)** that share the divine power of Lord Venkateswara:

  1. Dhruva Bera (Moola Virat): The main 8-foot tall self-manifested granite deity standing fixed in the sanctum. He receives the daily *Abhishekam* on Fridays and remains immovable.
  2. Bhoga Srinivasa (Kautuka Bera): A exquisite 1-foot silver idol gifted by Pallava Queen Samavai in 966 CE. Connected to the Moola Virat by a silk thread, He receives daily morning *Abhishekam* and night *Ekanta Seva* in a silver cradle.
  3. Malayappa Swamy (Utsava Bera): The radiant brass deity discovered in a hill cave (*Malayappan Konai*). Accompanied by Sridevi and Bhudevi, Malayappa Swamy goes out in procession for all public festivals, Brahmotsavams, and street processions.
  4. Ugra Srinivasa (Snapana Bera): The original procession deity of ancient times. According to legend, when He was carried out during daylight, fires broke out in the temple. Thus, He is taken out in procession only once a year before sunrise on *Kaisika Dwadashi*.
  5. Koluvu Srinivasa (Bali Bera): The deity who sits in daily court (*Koluvu*) every morning in the Ananda Nilayam. He listens to the previous day's financial accounts, hundi collections, and governs daily temple administration.

Fact-check note: Nanduri Srinivas's analysis of Pancha Beras reveals how Vaikhanasa Agama distributes administrative, ceremonial, and contemplative energies across five distinct forms.

The Ananda Nilayam Miracles

The sanctum sanctorum, crowned by the gold-gilded **Ananda Nilayam Vimanam**, harbors attributes that have fascinated devotees, saints, and scientists alike for centuries. The Moola Virat deity maintains a constant temperature, and despite elaborate camphor (*Pacha Karpooram*) applications — a chemical substance known to cause stone corrosion — the granite deity remains completely unblemished and smooth.

Devotees and priests who stand near the back of the Moola Virat report hearing the rhythmic sound of cosmic ocean waves (*Ksheerabdi*) echoing softly from the deity's back. Whether viewed through mystic faith or reverent wonder, Ananda Nilayam remains the supreme heart of South Indian devotional life.

Core Wisdom
  • • Vaksha Sthalam: Sri Devi and Padmavati reside eternally on the Lord's chest as mercy and grace.
  • • Pancha Beras: Five distinct manifestations managing contemplation, ritual, procession, and governance.
  • • Bhoga Srinivasa: A thousand-year-old silver link connecting daily worship to the eternal Moola Virat.
  • • Ananda Nilayam: The golden vimana that shelters the cosmic energy of Venkatadri.

Devotees, Saints, and Miracles Across Ages

భక్త శిఖరాగ్రాలు & అద్భుతాలు • History and Devotional Lineages

Tirumala — Lord Venkateswara accepting simple clay flowers from Kurmathi Nambi over gold flowers.

Tondaman Chakravarthi & Kurmathi Nambi’s Clay Flowers

The earliest temple constructor remembered in puranic tradition is **King Tondaman**, brother of Akasa Raja. Guided by Lord Srinivasa in a dream, Tondaman built the original temple, planted flower gardens, and constructed the Ananda Nilayam tower. Daily, Tondaman offered exquisite golden lotus flowers to Lord Venkateswara during worship.

However, one morning when Tondaman entered the sanctum, he was stunned to see that his golden lotuses had been moved aside, and the Lord's feet were adorned with crude, handmade **clay lotus flowers**! Troubled, the king searched the realm and discovered a poor, unlettered potter named **Kurmathi Nambi** living at the foot of the hill.

Nambi, unable to climb the hill due to poverty and age, spent his days shaping clay pots, constantly chanting "Govinda, Govinda" in his heart. As he worked, he crafted small lotus flowers out of wet clay and mentally offered them at the feet of Venkateswara. Lord Venkateswara accepted Nambi's humble clay flowers offered with pure love over the emperor's gold lotuses! Tondaman fell at Nambi's feet, learning that God seeks **Bhakti (devotion)**, not ostentatious wealth.

Traditional layer: Retold with deep emotional nuance in Chaganti Koteswara Rao's Venkatachala Vaibhavam.

Bhagavad Ramanujacharya — Standardizing Agama & Symbols

Tirumala — Sri Ramanujacharya presenting the sacred Shankha and Chakra to Lord Venkateswara.

In the 11th-12th century CE, the great Vishishtadvaita philosopher **Bhagavad Ramanujacharya** visited Tirumala. At that time, sectarian disputes had arisen regarding whether the deity on the hill was Vishnu, Shiva, or Shakti, as the ancient stone icon did not visibly display weapons.

Ramanujacharya proposed a divine test: he placed weapons belonging to different deities (the Shankha and Chakra of Vishnu, the Trident of Shiva, etc.) inside the locked sanctum overnight. When the doors were opened at dawn, the deity was found gloriously holding the golden **Shankha and Chakra** crafted by Ramanuja! Ramanujacharya established the **Vaikhanasa Agama** ritual code, laid out the Yamunothari flower gardens under his disciple Ananthalwar, appointed the Jeeyangar Mutt for daily spiritual oversight, and ordained that every human being regardless of caste could receive darshan.

Historical layer: Recorded in Guruparamparai texts & medieval Tirumala inscriptions.

Tallapaka Annamacharya — 32,000 Sankeertanas

In the 15th century, the saint-poet **Tallapaka Annamacharya (Annamayya)** arrived at Tirumala from Tallapaka village. Overwhelmed by the beauty of Lord Venkateswara and Mother Alamelu Manga, Annamayya spent his entire life composing **32,000 Sankeertanas** (devotional songs) in rich Telugu and Sanskrit.

His songs — including timeless classics like "Brahmamokkate Parabrahmamokkate" (All human beings are equal before God), "Nigama Nigamanta", and "Kondalalo Keloona" — pioneered South Indian Carnatic and devotional literature. Annamayya engraved his songs on copper plates, which were safely preserved in the *Sankeertana Bhandagaram* inside the Tirumala temple for centuries.

Tarigonda Vengamamba & Saint Hathiramji Bavaji

Hathiramji Mutt — Saint Hathiramji playing the game of dice with Lord Venkateswara.

In the 18th century, the mystic poetess **Tarigonda Vengamamba** lived on Tirumala hill. Despite severe societal opposition, her unshakeable love for Venkateswara moved the Lord to accept her daily offering of food and pearl harati. To this day, the final night Harati offered to Lord Venkateswara during *Ekanta Seva* is called **Tarigonda Vengamamba Mutyala Harati**!

Around the same era, a North Indian saint named **Hathiramji Bavaji** came to Tirumala. So pure was Hathiramji's devotion that Lord Venkateswara would manifest in human form every night inside Hathiramji's cottage to play the game of dice (**Chaupar**). One night, engrossed in the game, the Lord left His priceless diamond necklace in Hathiramji's hut. When priests discovered the necklace missing, Hathiramji was imprisoned by the local ruler. To prove his innocence, the ruler locked Hathiramji in a room filled with tons of sugarcane, demanding he eat it all overnight. Hathiramji prayed, and Lord Venkateswara entered the room as a giant elephant (Hathi), consumed all the sugarcane in minutes, and broke open the doors — establishing the famous **Hathiramji Mutt**!

Core Wisdom
  • • Clay Flowers: The Lord values sincerity of heart over material opulence.
  • • Ramanuja's Reform: Restoring Agama order and opening divine grace to all humanity.
  • • Annamayya's Voice: Music as the highest vehicle for social equality and divine love.
  • • Hathiramji & Vengamamba: God plays with those whose devotion transcends all social boundaries.

Living Niti & Pilgrimage Culture Today

నిత్య సేవలు & బ్రహ్మోత్సవాలు • Worship, Prasadam, and Garuda Seva

Tirumala Mada Streets — Lord Malayappa Swamy riding the golden Garuda Vahanam during Brahmotsavam.

The Daily Worship Cycle — From Suprabhatam to Ekanta Seva

Every day at Tirumala follows an intricate, unbroken ritual cycle governed by Vaikhanasa Agama. At 3:00 AM, the sanctum awakens with the chanting of **Sri Venkateswara Suprabhatam** (composed by Prativadi Bhayankaram Annan in the 15th century): "Kausalya supraja Rama purva sandhya pravartate..."

This is followed by *Tomala Seva* (adornment with fresh flower garlands brought from Yamunothari garden), *Archana* (chanting the 1,000 names of Vishnu), *Kalyanotsavam* (celestial wedding ceremony), *Koluvu* (daily court and accounting), and concludes late at night with **Ekanta Seva**, where Bhoga Srinivasa is lulled to sleep with Annamayya's lullaby "Jovu Achyutananda Jo Jo Mukunda".

Kalyanakatta & The Srivari Hundi

Millions of pilgrims climb the hills every year to perform **Tonsure (Kalyanakatta)** — shaving their head completely as a public offering. This practice represents the surrender of human vanity, ego, and physical beauty at the feet of the Lord. The hair is offered to Neela Devi, and the gesture leaves the pilgrim refreshed, humbled, and spiritually reborn.

Following tonsure and bath in Swami Pushkarini, pilgrims enter the queue complex leading to the sanctum. Passing before the Lord for a few breathtaking seconds of **Darshan**, they place their offerings inside the giant cloth-lined **Srivari Hundi**. Coins, currency, gold, and handwritten prayers fall into the hundi in a continuous river of gratitude.

Srivari Laddu Prasadam & Free Annaprasadam

No journey to Tirumala is complete without receiving **Srivari Laddu Prasadam** — the world-famous sweet crafted inside the temple kitchen (**Padi Potu**) using pure cow ghee, cashew nuts, raisins, cardamom, and sugar candy (*Kalkandu*). Awarded a Geographical Indication (GI) tag, the Laddu is regarded not as food, but as tangible divine grace (*Bhagavat Prasadam*).

Alongside the Laddu stands the massive **Tarigonda Vengamamba Annaprasadam Complex**, where over 100,000 pilgrims are served hot, delicious, free vegetarian meals daily with untiring hospitality — exemplifying the ancient Vedic ideal *Atithi Devo Bhava* (The guest is God).

Srivari Brahmotsavam & Garuda Seva

The annual high point of Tirumala worship is the nine-day **Srivari Brahmotsavam**, celebrated during Navaratri in September-October. According to legend, Lord Brahma himself descends to Earth to initiate these festival celebrations for Lord Venkateswara.

On the fifth night of Brahmotsavam occurs the grand **Garuda Seva**. Procession deity **Lord Malayappa Swamy**, adorned with priceless royal jewels including the *Makarakanthi* and *Sahasranama Haram*, rides the majestic golden **Garuda Vahanam** around the four Mada Streets surrounding the temple. Hundreds of thousands of pilgrims fill every inch of the streets, chanting in one unified, echoing roar: "GOVINDA! GOVINDA! GOVINDA!"
